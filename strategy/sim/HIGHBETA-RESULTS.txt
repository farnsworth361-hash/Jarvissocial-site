                                                  THE FINDING                                                   
================================================================================================================
Look at the P(profit) column. Every one of these sits at 21-22%.

Delta-matching standardises the bet. A 30-delta option is a 30-delta
option whether the underlying moves 1% a day or 5% a day -- the higher
IV is already in the price. Buying a 'more volatile' name at the same
delta does NOT raise your odds of winning. The market is not offering
free movement.

What DOES change, and is worth having:

  SPY  785/790   target is  40.8% of max profit;  1σ(44d) =   3.7%;  needs  +2.6% (0.70σ)
  NVDA 240/245   target is  32.5% of max profit;  1σ(44d) =  11.3%;  needs  +7.0% (0.62σ)
  PLTR 185/190   target is  34.2% of max profit;  1σ(44d) =  16.0%;  needs  +9.2% (0.57σ)
  TSLA 390/395   target is  31.6% of max profit;  1σ(44d) =  14.7%;  needs  +9.2% (0.63σ)
  MSTR 140/145   target is  28.2% of max profit;  1σ(44d) =  24.8%;  needs +13.6% (0.55σ)

The $5 width is a far smaller slice of a volatile name's expected move,
so the debit is cheaper relative to the width and the reward:risk is
better. NVDA pays 3.08:1 against SPY's 2.45:1, and its +100% target is
32.5% of max profit versus SPY's 40.8% -- a materially easier bar.

That is the real trade: same win probability, bigger payoff when right,
bigger loss when wrong, and 4-5 extra points of break-even hit rate
handed to the market maker in wider ticks. Worth it only if you accept
the variance -- it is not a free upgrade.
